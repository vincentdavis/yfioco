Experience & Staff

The crew is the product

Vending and merch are simple to describe and hard to run well. What separates a smooth night from an expensive one is who is standing behind the table — and whether they have done it before.

What experience actually buys you

Not a smoother pitch. Three specific things that show up on the night.

Fewer surprises

The load-in that starts an hour late. The card reader that drops mid-rush. The merch that arrives right before doors in the wrong sizes. None of it is new to us, so none of it becomes your problem to solve at 7pm.

Faster lines

A seller who knows the layout, the price points, and where the restock sits moves a line at a different pace than one learning all three during the opener. Line speed is revenue, and it is almost entirely a staffing question.

Numbers that reconcile

Counts done the same way, by people trained to do them that way, every event. That is the whole reason settlement is boring instead of contentious.

The people we send

We staff events the same way each time, because consistency is the thing venues actually buy from us.

  • A named lead on every event. One person owns the count and is your single point of contact from load-in to settlement.
  • The same crew, where we can. Sending back people who already know your room beats sending strangers who have to learn it.
  • Trained before doors, not during them. Sellers know the product and the prices before the first guest reaches the table.
  • One procedure everywhere. Cash handling and point of sale work the same at every event we run, so nothing depends on who happened to be scheduled.
  • Setup and teardown included. Part of the job, not a separate ask at the end of a long night.

What every seller is trained on

  • Point of sale and cash handling
  • Count-in and count-out procedure
  • Artist and promoter settlement
  • Restocking mid-rush without closing the line
  • Venue rules and the licenses the sale requires
  • Guest interaction — for two minutes, they are your venue

Why it matters to your venue

A merch table is a guest-facing counter with your name behind it. When the line is slow, the seller is rude, or the count does not add up, none of that reads to a guest or an artist as a vendor problem. It reads as a venue problem.

Which is why we treat staffing as the service rather than a cost inside it. The product mix and the display matter. The person behind the table matters more.

Staffing on short notice

A vendor falling through the week of an event is one of the more common reasons venues call us. If that is where you are right now, say so in the first line of your email and we will tell you quickly whether we can cover it.
